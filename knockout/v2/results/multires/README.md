# Coarse-to-fine resynthesis on `second-autocorrelation-inequality` (2026-11-14, evening PT)

Third method of Knockout v2, next to the CPU moves and the fixed-resolution GPU annealing chains: pool a solution to
a coarser grid, optimize there, repeat-upsample, perturb, optimize at the next level, up to 2,000,000 cells. Code:
`knockout/v2/multires/`. It reuses the batched float64 chains of `knockout/v2/gpu/` (f = w², LogSumExp maximum,
batched L-BFGS) as the optimizer of every level. Tests: `tests/test_knockout_v2_multires.py` (18 tests, no torch).

**Result in one line: no record, and nothing here beats the plain polisher.** The best exact score is
0.9636616206516342, the plain polish of the live #1 (+5.86e-8 over the live #1, where a record needs +1e-5). Every
coarse-to-fine variant ends below the solution it started from; the best one ends 6.1e-5 below.

Every score below is the frozen verifier's float64 score on CPU unless it says "GPU closed form" (only for the
4,000,000-cell level of E6, which the frozen scorer refuses). Full tables, one per batch: [`TABLES.md`](TABLES.md)
(generated by `python -m knockout.v2.multires.report knockout/v2/results/multires`). Records:
`<run_id>/levels.jsonl` (shared JSONL format plus the WS3 fields), `<run_id>/summary.json`.

Reference scores: frozen certificate = EA solution 2782 = 0.963638667992814; live #1 = EA solution 2783
(MLAI_YONSEI) = 0.963661562062085, read on 2026-11-15 05:45 UTC; δ = 1e-5.

## 1. Sources and recipes

Web pages, papers and repositories were read for the method only; no code from them was run, and every score
quoted from them is a claim.

| # | Recipe | Read in the source | My adaptation |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Repeat, then polish | `np.repeat(f, k)` keeps the score exactly; records were reached by duplicating (524k → 1.05M / 1.57M) and polishing, then zero-padding to the cap; "double the solution via np.repeat, then Dinkelbach polish with aggressive beta annealing up to 1e11"; β cascade 1e5 → 1e9 at each resolution. Sources: [ClaudeExplorer repository](https://github.com/justinkang221/second-autocorrelation-inequality), its [Hyra campaign notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/justinkang221/second-autocorrelation-inequality/main/experiments/12_hyra_campaign/NOTES.md), [EinsteinArena thread 180](https://einsteinarena.com/problems/second-autocorrelation-inequality/threads/180) | The level optimizer is our batched L-BFGS on −log C_β instead of Dinkelbach. Each level cools from a first β (1e1 … 1e6, 3 stages per decade) to 1e9; the last level polishes to 1e11. The coarse start is the incumbent sum-pooled (the sources only go upwards; thread 180 reports a 1.6M → 100k average-pool transplant at 0.96221 and, in a reply, 6.4M → 1.6M failing) |
| R2 | Coarse to fine with noise | Boyer and Li start on 23 intervals and alternate gradient ascent and upsampling up to 575 ([arXiv:2506.16750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16750), method as described in [arXiv:2508.02803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02803)); Jaech and Joseph run noisy Adam (gradient noise η/(t+1)^γ, η = 1e-3, γ = 0.65), then upsample 4× by interpolation and run gradient ascent with clipping ([arXiv:2508.02803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02803)). They also warn that an upsampled coarse optimum "may inherit the same local maximum" | A ladder of doublings from 31,250 (or 500,000, or 1,000,000) cells to 2,000,000; the noise is a perturbation after every upsampling (log-normal of width 0.1 / 0.3 / 1.0, several seeds) instead of gradient noise inside Adam |
| R3 | Velocity cycles | `f_pert = f_best + 0.3 (f_best − f_prev) + U(0, 1e-8 max f)`, then a polish with β = 1e6 … 1e14 ([accelerated_optimizer.py and run_velocity_continue.py](https://github.com/justinkang221/second-autocorrelation-inequality/tree/main/experiments/12_hyra_campaign)) | The uniform all-cell noise is one row of the V-cycles; the momentum direction is the line through solutions 2782 and 2783 (E5) |
| — | Lineage of the records | 5e4 → 1e5 → 4e5 intervals by doubling and local refinement, Dinkelbach plus simulated annealing ([arXiv:2606.10402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10402), Appendix C) | background only |

The notes of the ClaudeExplorer repository also list measured failures that match what we see: "every transform of
an incumbent solution falls off the manifold; only descent-based polish makes progress".

Every redraw costs 2e-2 to 5e-1 at once, even on 1 % of the blocks (`local_measurements.json`), and the chain
comes back to a point below its start. The GPU annealing agent reached the same conclusion with kicks, so this was
not repeated. The planned support-edge opening (blind, then guided by the derivative; plans `edge` and `grow`) was
measured on CPU only: opening the cells next to the support at t times the neighbouring value costs 1.3 × t at
once (`local_measurements.json`); selecting the cells with a positive derivative at β = 1e6 cuts that cost
160-fold, but the smoothed objective then gains only 2e-9 (measured once with a scratch script, not stored). Its
GPU batch was stopped by hand when the annealing agent reported that opening cells always loses.

## 6. How solution 2783 differs from 2782 (verified measurements, `local_measurements.json`, `repetition_traces.json`, `vcycle_vs_public.json`, `line_*.json`)

- Same support for 95 % of the cells: 23,191 cells were born (0.77 % of the mass) and 25,554 died; 75 % of the born
  cells are within 2 cells of the old support, 94 % within 8. The comb teeth moved their edges.
- **No trace of a block repetition** in either solution: 0.025 % of neighbouring cells are equal (75.7 % in a
  repeated control); steps inside groups of r cells are as large as steps across groups for r = 2 … 32 (ratio 0.95
  to 1.00; 0 in the control); run starts fall evenly on the residues modulo r; and the distance between the two
  mass profiles after aggregation by r decreases smoothly for every r from 1 to 64 (0.225, 0.182, 0.159, 0.145, …,
  0.046), with no factor at which they suddenly agree.
- **2783 is much closer to 2782 than a re-derived solution is.** The best V-cycle end V (depth 2, derived from
  2782) is as far from 2782 cell by cell as 2783 is (relative L1 0.18 against 0.23), with a closer envelope (0.003
  against 0.009). Yet the exact score on the straight line from 2782 dips to 0.9311 halfway to V and only to
  0.9609 halfway to 2783 (12 times less), and 1 % of the way it loses 1.3e-3 towards V against 1.1e-4 towards
  2783. The line from 2783 to V is as rough as the line from 2782 to V (0.9304 halfway).
- Hypothesis drawn from the last two points: solution 2783 was **not** re-derived from a coarser level or from the
  envelope; it is a continuation of 2782 (many small accepted steps, as in the public velocity cycles), which
  drifts far along directions that cost little and stays compatible with 2782. This is against the reading that
  motivated this work.
- The whole gain of 2783 is in the plateau term: 0.036320 against 0.036344 (−2.4e-5), with a slightly larger
  roughness term (1.83e-5 against 1.69e-5).

Each launch is one H100 container (the function caps itself at 2, but two launches are two apps: launch at most two
at a time). GPU kernels are not bit-deterministic: a result is reproduced from its stored solution, not from a

own start (`@live`) and its own evaluations (`=12000`).

## 12. What is verified, what is a hypothesis, what is left

- Verified: every score in the tables (frozen verifier, CPU, float64); the invariances; the measurements of
  section 6; the negative outcome of every variant, from solution 2782 and, for the V-cycle, the lines and the
  polish, from solution 2783.
- Hypothesis: the reading of how 2783 was produced (section 6).
- Not tested: whether a much longer or slower cooling at the coarse level would close the remaining 6e-5. The loss
  at 1,000,000 cells went from 1.0e-4 to 7.5e-5 when the evaluations doubled, and 6 stages per decade instead of 3
  gained another 4e-6; the fine level then adds about 1e-5.
- Limits: one seed for most rows without noise (they are deterministic up to GPU rounding); 8,000 to 16,000
  evaluations per level; the level optimizer cannot open a cell (only floors and perturbations do); E3b and E3c
  (opening cells at the edge of the support) were planned and coded but not run on the GPU.
- Side effects in the repository: two shared logs grew, `selection/claims.jsonl` (three arbiter verdicts) and
  `selection/arena_log.jsonl` (the read-only GETs of the live #1).

What I would try next (none of it tested here):

1. Continue instead of re-deriving. The evidence of section 6 says solution 2783 continues 2782. Reproduce the
   public velocity cycles as published (Dinkelbach inner problem with Anderson acceleration, β up to 1e14, uniform
   noise of 1e-8 max f on every cell, momentum 0.3) for 50 to 100 cycles from solution 2783 and measure the gain per
   cycle. Our polisher minimizes −log C_β with β ≤ 1e11 and stops at +5.9e-8; whether their inner solver moves
   where ours does not is the open question.
2. A solver for the true minimax problem, with the 374,000 lags within 1e-7 of the maximum as an active set
   (sequential linear programming, or a primal-dual method with FFT operators), in which cells can open and close.
   Every method tried today smooths the maximum with a LogSumExp, and opens cells only through floors or kicks.
3. Not worth more budget, on today's evidence: deeper V-cycles, noise after the upsampling, block redraws, lines
   towards a V-cycle solution, and work above the cap.
